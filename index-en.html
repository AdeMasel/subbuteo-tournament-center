What it is

Not a spreadsheet.
A broadcast gallery.

A single program, in a single file, that needs no Internet and asks for no account. On one side it does the arithmetic — fixtures, points, tie-breaks, brackets, referees, tables. On the other it puts them on stage: glowing scoreboards, running clocks, a scrolling ticker, a voice calling players to their tables, music fading as the countdown begins. In the tournament hall you can see the difference.

Everything it does

From the draw to the trophy

Fourteen formats, and something to tell you which one to pick

Groups with knockout and lucky losers, groups with final tiers, league (single or double round), knockout with a consolation bracket, Swiss system, playoffs with the Page system and play-outs, a Champions-style league phase, double elimination, “King of the Table”, and team tournaments.

Not sure which to use? The Format Adviser looks at how many of you there are and offers all the workable options — multi-stage ones included — with total matches and duration, then applies them to the Setup in one click.

  • Real Tournament: 18 official competitions (Serie A, Premier League, LaLiga, Champions League, the 2026 World Cup, Libertadores…) with their exact format and authentic logo
  • Re-orderable tie-break criteria, configurable points, seeds and pots
  • Squads, players, captains and 5,037 crests of clubs and national teams, ready to use

The draw as a ceremony

The crystal bowl, the balls tumbling, the card turning in mid-air to reveal the crest, the group lighting up. At the end the golden tickets fly across the room with real metallic glints.

It respects the pots (seeds, second pot and so on) and distributes exactly as the rules require; you can draw by hand, one team at a time, or let it run on its own. Then one button turns the draw into the official group composition.

What time do we finish? It tells you before you start

Choose the format, the match length and the start time: the program counts the matches, works out how many waves you need with the tables you actually have, and gives you back the expected finishing time.

  • Break between rounds, adjustable from 4 to 10 minutes
  • Lunch break up to 80 minutes
  • Fifteen minutes for the prize-giving
  • The breakdown line by line: matches, waves, playing time, breaks

Double the tables and watch the finish time move back by three hours. It is the quickest way to see whether the format you have in mind fits inside the day.

The tournament live, table by table

The Match Center is one board per table: an independent stopwatch (1–60 minutes, plus extra time), a simulated clock running from 0′ to 90′, scorers by the minute, cards and penalty flicks. A shared START with a countdown, and the whistle at full time.

  • Automatic table assignment: one button fills every free table in fixture order, never double-booking anyone, and if a table would sit empty it brings forward a match from the next round
  • Referee management fair and incremental: referees are drawn from the entered players or from a dedicated list, the name appears on the board, in the fixture list and in the spoken announcement, with a tally of who has already officiated and how much
  • S.O.S. from the table: the referee requests a stoppage from their phone, the gallery grants or refuses it, and play resumes only when the person at the desk says so
  • Free matches, changes mid-tournament, up to 4 simultaneous tournaments

Team table football, exactly as the rules require

A tie is four simultaneous matches across four tables, and the pairing follows the official FISCT procedure, reproduced step by step: handing in the team sheet, the coin toss (or home advantage in return legs), then the alternating line-up in five moves 1-2-2-2-1.

The program referees the procedure itself: it says whose move it is, highlights in red the table where the opponent is exposed, enforces the required cover when it is owed, accepts only the permitted tables and knows how to step back. At the interval it opens the window for the two substitutions, respecting the priority set by the draw.

Four new formats: nobody goes home early

La 6.3 brings in the formats used by the big Italian tournaments. The Mirror Format: every player enters a bracket chosen by where they finished in their group — winners into Master Gold, runners-up into Silver, all the way down to Finisher — and inside every bracket the loser keeps playing, with finals for 1st-2nd, 3rd-4th, 5th-6th and 7th-8th. Every position is settled on the table.

Then the Cross-Group Ranking to build the second phase on overall merit when the groups are not the same size; the Second Group Phase for tournaments of 32, 44, 48 and 52 entrants, with an Upper Section and Chaltrons; and the Cross Finals, where two parallel competitions meet only at the very end, equal placing against equal placing.

  • In the Mirror Format everyone plays the same number of matches
  • Players who already met in the group stage do not meet again in the second phase
  • The tiers adjust themselves to the number of entrants: nobody is left out through an oversight
  • Fourteen formats in all, from a single league to tournaments of 52 players

Three formats that were missing, and a result you can undo

La two-legged cup: every round over two matches, decided on aggregate, with the table reversed in the return leg and a single-match final. The McMahon system: a Swiss that does not start from zero, where the stronger players begin ahead and the best meet each other from the first round — born in Go tournaments, it is the right answer when entrants are many and of uneven standard. The Olympic repechage: the judo formula, where anyone beaten by a finalist comes back into contention, so nobody leaves the tournament because of an unlucky draw.

And at last you can undo a result: the match goes back among those still to be played, with its report and cards cleared. Before doing it the program tells you what will be lost — and, in a bracket, how many already-played matches will be swept away, because whoever had gone through comes back.

  • Two bronzes in the repechage: two different routes to the same step of the podium
  • Over two legs, level on aggregate goes to penalty flicks: no away goals, as in today's football
  • Every cancellation leaves a trace in a log: a dispute has to be reconstructable
  • Le photographs sent by referees now really arrive: a defect was blocking all of them, with or without the tunnel

The goal is already saved before you think of recording it

The goal always arrives a moment before you press “record”. The new Replay App turns the problem around: a phone points at the table and films continuously, but saves nothing — it holds in memory only the last eight seconds. When something happens you press the red button and those eight seconds you have just watched become an animated GIF, which sets off on its own to the gallery already matched to the match.

  • You record after watching: there is nothing left to anticipate
  • The clip is a GIF: it opens anywhere, weighs a few hundred kilobytes, no codecs
  • At full time the gallery edits on its own every incident into a single film, on your computer, with no external software and no Internet
  • Continuous filming up to 30 minutes, then it switches itself off so as not to drain the battery
  • It connects by scanning a QR code, like the Table App

The winner's page

When the last result decides the tournament, the screen lights up: the crest in a golden frame, the team and player name in metallic gold, the podium with its medals and the fireworks rising, bursting and falling in sparks — with the champion's anthem starting by itself. You can play it again from the Standings at the moment of the presentation.

Streaming graphics

Streaming your tournament on YouTube or Twitch? The program serves a page with a transparent background to drop into OBS as a browser source: over the shot of the table appear crests, score and clock, all updated from the gallery. A lower third, a corner box, or full screen for the intervals.

🚪

When something goes wrong

A player gets injured or has to leave: the withdrawal settles all of their remaining matches in one go, by the rule you choose, without touching those already played. And while the tournament runs, a safety copy is written every five minutes to a folder on the computer: you can go back to any moment of the day.

👁

Readable by everyone

The zones of the table are not told apart by colour alone — green and red look identical to one person in twelve — but also by a symbol: ▲ through, ◆ playoff, ▽ play-out, ▼ relegated. Interface in Italian and English, 14 visual themes, and type designed to be read from the back row.

💾

Your data stays yours

Everything lives on your computer: no account, no subscription service, no data collection. A tournament exports to a file and reopens on another machine. Up to four simultaneous tournaments in the same program, with the tables shared between them.

Questions

What everyone asks

Is the licence valid on more than one computer?
No: the code is signed for that device and will not work on another. If you change computer, just ask for a new code giving the new identifier — and from the panel you can always remove the licence from the machine you no longer use.
Do I need the Internet to use it?
No. The program works completely offline, licence included: the code is checked locally at every start. The Internet is needed only if you want to connect the referees' phones when the venue has no shared Wi-Fi — and in that case one switch is enough: the program carries everything it needs and opens a free tunnel by itself.
Do the referees' phones need to install an app?
No. The Table App, the Captain App and the Replay App are web pages served by the gallery computer: scan a QR code and you are in. They work on Android and iPhone, in Italian and English, and they queue events if the network drops.
What happens to tournaments played in the demo version?
In the demo the tournament lives only while the window stays open: saving is disabled. As soon as you activate the licence, saving starts again and from that moment everything is kept on your computer.
macOS says the application cannot be opened
The installer is not signed with a paid Apple certificate. On first launch do a right-click on the icon → Open and confirm: from then on it opens normally. On Windows, if the blue SmartScreen page appears, choose More info → Run anyway.
Why is the installer so large?
Because inside there is everything: the music library, the 5,037 crests, the flags, the three complete rulebooks, the 45 original tracks of the music library and the logos of the official competitions. Nothing is downloaded later, so in the tournament hall it works with no network at all.
Can I use it for official federation tournaments?
Yes: the formats, the ranking criteria and the pairing procedure for team table football follow the FISCT/FISTF rules. The program remains an independent tool, however, and is not a federation product.
