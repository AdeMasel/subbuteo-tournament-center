What it is

Not a spreadsheet.
A broadcast Control Desk.

A single program, in a single file, that needs no Internet and asks for no account. On one side it does the arithmetic — fixtures, points, tie-breaks, brackets, referees, tables. On the other it puts them on stage: glowing scoreboards, running clocks, a scrolling ticker, a voice calling players to their tables, music fading as the countdown begins. In the tournament hall you can see the difference.

The draw as it looks on television

A studio draw: the presenter picks the ball, opens it and shows the slip, where the real name of the team drawn appears, handwritten. The voice says it over the studio sound; beside it a board fills up group by group. At the end, the applause.

It respects the pots (seeds, second pot and so on) and can keep club-mates apart, one per group. You can draw by hand, one team at a time, or let it run on its own. Then one button turns the draw into the official group composition.

What time do we finish? It tells you before you start

Choose the format, the match length and the start time: the program counts the matches, works out how many waves you need with the tables you actually have, and gives you back the expected finishing time.

  • Break between rounds, adjustable from 4 to 10 minutes
  • Lunch break up to 80 minutes
  • Fifteen minutes for the prize-giving
  • The breakdown line by line: matches, waves, playing time, breaks

Double the tables and watch the finish time move back by three hours. It is the quickest way to see whether the format you have in mind fits inside the day.

Team table football, exactly as the rules require

A tie is four simultaneous matches across four tables, and the pairing follows the official FISCT procedure, reproduced step by step: handing in the team sheet, the coin toss (or home advantage in return legs), then the alternating line-up in five moves 1-2-2-2-1. With the FISTF format, Coach A and Coach B choose game by game instead, as the international regulations require.

The program referees the procedure itself: it says whose move it is, highlights in red the table where the opponent is exposed, enforces the required cover when it is owed, accepts only the permitted tables and knows how to step back. At the interval it opens the window for the two substitutions, respecting the priority set by the draw.

Four new formats: nobody goes home early

Version 6.3 brings in the formats used by the big Italian tournaments. The Mirror Format: every player enters a bracket chosen by where they finished in their group — winners into Master Gold, runners-up into Silver, all the way down to Finisher — and inside every bracket the loser keeps playing, with finals for 1st-2nd, 3rd-4th, 5th-6th and 7th-8th. Every position is settled on the table.

Then the Cross-Group Ranking to build the second phase on overall merit when the groups are not the same size; the Second Group Phase for tournaments of 32, 44, 48 and 52 entrants, with an Upper Section and Chaltrons; and the Cross Finals, where two parallel competitions meet only at the very end, equal placing against equal placing.

  • In the Mirror Format everyone plays the same number of matches
  • Players who already met in the group stage do not meet again in the second phase
  • The tiers adjust themselves to the number of entrants: nobody is left out through an oversight
  • Twenty-one formats today, from a single league to the FISTF regulations

Three formats that were missing, and a result you can undo

La two-legged cup: every round over two matches, decided on aggregate, with the table reversed in the return leg and a single-match final. The McMahon system: a Swiss that does not start from zero, where the stronger players begin ahead and the best meet each other from the first round — born in Go tournaments, it is the right answer when entrants are many and of uneven standard. The Olympic repechage: the judo formula, where anyone beaten by a finalist comes back into contention, so nobody leaves the tournament because of an unlucky draw.

And at last you can undo a result: the match goes back among those still to be played, with its report and cards cleared. Before doing it the program tells you what will be lost — and, in a bracket, how many already-played matches will be swept away, because whoever had gone through comes back.

  • Two bronzes in the repechage: two different routes to the same step of the podium
  • Over two legs, level on aggregate goes to penalty flicks: no away goals, as in today's football
  • Every cancellation leaves a trace in a log: a dispute has to be reconstructable
  • Le photographs sent by referees now really arrive: a defect was blocking all of them, with or without the tunnel

The goal is already saved before you think of recording it

The goal always arrives a moment before you press “record”. The new Replay App turns the problem around: a phone points at the table and films continuously, but saves nothing — it holds in memory only the last eight seconds. When something happens you press the red button and those eight seconds you have just watched become a video, which sets off on its own to the Control Desk already matched to the match and, within seconds, goes on air on the audience screen.

  • You record after watching: there is nothing left to anticipate
  • The clip is a 720p MP4 video: it opens on any computer or phone
  • New Replay Room: the Control Desk's slow-motion desk, with incoming actions, favourites and the Put on air button
  • With the Presentation or AUTO LIVE running, the action interrupts the rotation, goes on air by itself and then hands the screen back
  • At full time the Control Desk edits on its own every incident into a single film, on your computer, with no external software and no Internet
  • Continuous filming up to 30 minutes, then it switches itself off so as not to drain the battery
  • It connects by scanning a QR code, like the Table App

A world to conquer, and stickers of the players

The World Map Cup is a campaign format for 13–24 players: the draw gives everyone a national team and a secret mission, and the world is divided into 24 territories across six confederations. Every win conquers a territory and the map fills with flags. Three phases — Borders, Expeditions, Capitals — and nobody is eliminated: six games each, then the Cup final and the bronze final.

Missions are no longer handed over out loud: from the map you open a player's personal QR code, they scan it and the mission appears on their phone only, covered until they uncover it themselves. Then they confirm receipt with one tap.

The stickers are what stays afterwards: a real photograph in the club shirt, the background removed and replaced by the sky, the crest and the name. And at the end, the album to print.

  • The Sceptre of the World Map for whoever holds the most flags: capitals count double
  • Every QR code is a personal credential, valid only for that card and revocable: missions never pass through the audience screen
  • Missions stay hidden until the awards

  • PDF album by club, by group or in name order, nine stickers per page

The FISTF regulations, table by table

Three new formats bring the FISTF Tournament Organisers Handbook — the world federation's rules — into the program: groups with the official bracket, the Swiss system with a final stage and the team tournament. No adaptations: match order, ranking criteria, barrages and pairings are those of the document, and they cannot be changed in the Setup.

From 2 to 32 groups the bracket comes from the official table: who goes into barrages M1, M2…, who has direct entry, who meets whom all the way to the final. All 31 tables were checked one by one — and the check even found a misprint in the document, now corrected.

  • Draw by ranking pots, club-mates or compatriots in different groups
  • FISTF match order for groups of 3 to 10, with the special orders for club-mates
  • Head-to-head and a shoot-out for complete ties; U12, U16, U20 and Ladies categories with a maximum margin of 5–0
  • Swiss: four rounds, a bye worth 3–0, a 16-player bracket (1–16, 8–9, 4–13…) or an 8-player one
  • Teams: Coach A and Coach B game by game, teams of three allowed, one substitution
  • Withdrawal with the FISTF rule, and at first start a screen sums up everything new in the version

Formats with barrage, and a LIVE desk that guides you

Two formats with a barrage arrive in the Setup. With Groups + Barrage the overall group ranking is split into bands — Gold, Silver, Bronze… of the size you choose — and in each band the best go straight into the bracket, while the others play the barrage round: with 12 players, 4 straight to the quarter-finals and 8 in the barrage.

With Bands with Barrage play goes in cycles: each band its own group, then the last plays the first of the band below and the winner stays above. Promotion and relegation up to the last cycle, when the top of the Gold band is the champion.

  • Band sizes, number of cycles and places at stake of your choice, with a preview that tells you at once who goes straight through
  • In LIVE the buttons become 3D backlit keys, in every theme
  • The next step always blinks: Assign the fields, START ROUND, Next Phase, and End all in red
  • Once the matches are closed the program goes back to Groups or to the Final Stage by itself

Ten things you would not expect

🎛

Control Desk

The Control Desk: your computer becomes a public-address system — plug in a microphone and speak to the room through the speakers, with a red button when you are off and green when you are live, and the music ducking by itself while you talk. Forty-five original tracks by the author, included in the licence, with cross-fading between tracks. The two free announcements also go out from here: one scrolls on the ticker with an orange pill, the other is read out loud at regular intervals — the prize-giving that has moved, the bar closing, the car park to be cleared.

Offline neural voice

Announcements shouldn't sound like a 2005 satnav. The program can install three neural Italian voices — «Serena», female, high quality; «Paola», female; and «Riccardo», male — that run on your own computer: no internet during the tournament, no account, no cost, no limit on how much they say. They download once from the Control Desk, survive app updates, and are the same on Mac, Windows and Linux. Every announcement opens with the airport-style chime that gathers the room's attention, and background music ducks by itself under the voice, coming back up when it has finished. Below it, a panel reads the voices installed on your system and tells you whether you are already using a good one.

When something goes wrong

A player gets injured or has to leave: the withdrawal settles all of their remaining matches in one go, by the rule you choose, without touching those already played (in FISTF tournaments the regulation rule applies: 0–3 in every group match). And while the tournament runs, a safety copy is written every five minutes to a folder on the computer: you can go back to any moment of the day.

👁

Readable by everyone

The zones of the table are not told apart by colour alone — green and red look identical to one person in twelve — but also by a symbol: ▲ through, ◆ playoff, ▽ play-out, ▼ relegated. Interface in Italian and English, 21 visual themes, and type designed to be read from the back row.

💾

Your data stays yours

Everything lives on your computer: no account, no subscription service, no data collection. A tournament exports to a file and reopens on another machine. Up to four simultaneous tournaments in the same program, with the tables shared between them.

The program that works for you

The features that take away from the organiser the gestures that pin them to the desk: typing a score, copying out a phone number, answering “which table am I on?” for the twentieth time.

  • The score is dictated by voice. “Table two, three one” and the match is recorded. The app repeats aloud what it understood and waits for your confirmation: nothing is written behind your back
  • The ticker chooses what to say. While play is on it shows the scores; between rounds the next round with table and referee; when it matters the news — how much delay has built up, the finishing time recalculated on the real pace, the overtakes, the groups about to close. Questions

What everyone asks

Is the licence valid on more than one computer?
No: the code is signed for that device and will not work on another. If you change computer, just ask for a new code giving the new identifier — and from the panel you can always remove the licence from the machine you no longer use.
Do I need the Internet to use it?
No. The program works completely offline, licence included: the code is checked locally at every start. The Internet is needed only if you want to connect the referees' phones when the venue has no shared Wi-Fi — and in that case one switch is enough: the program carries everything it needs and opens a free tunnel by itself. And of course it is needed to go live on YouTube or TikTok.
How do I go live on YouTube or TikTok?
From the Control Desk, 🔴 Live on YouTube and TikTok section: paste once the stream key that YouTube Studio or TikTok (LIVE Center or LIVE Studio) gives to the account owner, choose the layout and press Go live. No other software and no user name or password are needed; the stream's title and visibility are set on the platform. On TikTok the key changes with every stream, and streaming from a computer is reserved for enabled accounts. Two 1080 streams need about 10 Mbps of upload: a network cable is better.
Do the referees' phones need to install an app?
No. The Table App, the Captain App and the Replay App are web pages served by the Control Desk computer: scan a QR code and you are in. They work on Android and iPhone, in Italian and English, and they queue events if the network drops.
What happens to tournaments played in the demo version?
In the demo the tournament lives only while the window stays open: saving is disabled. As soon as you activate the licence, saving starts again and from that moment everything is kept on your computer.
macOS says the application cannot be opened
The installer is not signed with a paid Apple certificate, which costs a yearly subscription. On first launch macOS warns that the developer is not identified. On macOS 15 Sequoia and later: double-click the app, then open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the bottom and press Open Anyway. On earlier versions a right-click on the icon → Open is enough. You only need to do it once: after that it opens like any other application. On Windows, if the blue SmartScreen page appears, choose More info → Run anyway.
Why is the installer so large?
Because inside there is everything: the 5,610 club badges, the 233 flags, the four complete rulebooks, the 45 original tracks of the music library and the logos of the official competitions. Nothing is downloaded later, so in the tournament hall it works with no network at all.
Can I use it for official federation tournaments?
Yes: since 9.0 three formats follow the FISTF Tournament Organisers Handbook to the letter — match order, ranking criteria, barrages and official brackets for 2 to 32 groups — and team table football has both the FISCT and the FISTF pairing procedures. The program remains an independent tool, however, and is not a federation product.
